Marie Curie was a Polish-born physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity.
Marie Curie was born in Warsaw and later moved to France. Marie Curie worked at the
University of Paris, where she became the first woman to hold a professorship. Marie Curie
married Pierre Curie in 1895. Pierre Curie was a French physicist who studied crystals and
magnetism. Marie Curie and Pierre Curie discovered the elements polonium and radium together
in their laboratory in Paris. In 1903, Marie Curie and Pierre Curie shared the Nobel Prize
in Physics with Henri Becquerel for their joint work on radiation.

Albert Einstein was a theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity. Albert
Einstein worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. Albert Einstein received
the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 for his explanation of the photoelectric effect. Albert
Einstein was a friend of Marie Curie, and both scientists attended the Solvay Conference in
Brussels.
