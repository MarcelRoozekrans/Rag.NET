# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith, including — especially — when you are pointing out that something
here is broken. Reporting a defect is the contribution this project values most, and nobody should
have to be diplomatic about a bug to be taken seriously.

## What is expected

- Treat people with respect, including when you disagree with them.
- Criticise the code, the claim, or the decision. Not the person who wrote it.
- Accept that you will sometimes be wrong in public. Everyone here will be, repeatedly; the project's
  own history files are largely a record of the maintainer being wrong and correcting it.
- Take a correction as information rather than an attack.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, whether public or private.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or personal attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions.
- Conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and code
review — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a problem to **marcel@roozekrans.nl**.

Reports are handled privately. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will not be asked to justify
why something bothered you before it is looked at. If the report involves the maintainer, say so —
it will still be read, and you are entitled to say it directly.

**Do not use a public issue.** That exposes the reporter, which is the one outcome worth avoiding.

Security vulnerabilities are a separate process: see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

Responses are proportionate, and range from a private word to a permanent ban from the project's
spaces. The maintainer decides, and will say what was decided and why to the people involved.

This is a small project maintained by one person. That means the process is informal, not that it is
absent — and it means the honest commitment is **best effort and a straight answer**, rather than a
service level this project cannot actually staff.
